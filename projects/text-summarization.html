Overview

This project implements an extractive text summarization system using a Genetic Algorithm (GA). Instead of relying on deep learning models, the system treats summarization as an optimization problem, where the goal is to select the most informative subset of words from the original document.

By evolving candidate summaries over multiple generations, the algorithm progressively improves summary quality while reducing overall text length.

Problem Statement

Large textual documents are difficult and time-consuming to read. Traditional summarization approaches often struggle to balance information retention with brevity.

The challenge addressed in this project is to automatically generate compact summaries that preserve key information using an algorithmic, optimization-driven approach.

Methodology & Approach

  • Preprocess and tokenize the input document
  • Represent candidate summaries as binary chromosomes
  • Initialize a population of random summaries
  • Evaluate fitness based on word relevance and coverage
  • Apply selection, crossover, and mutation operators
  • Evolve the population across multiple generations

Genetic Algorithm Design

  • Encoding: Binary representation indicating word inclusion
  • Selection: Roulette wheel selection based on fitness
  • Crossover: Single-point crossover to combine solutions
  • Mutation: Bit-flip mutation to introduce diversity
  • Fitness Function: Word overlap-based relevance scoring

This design allows the algorithm to efficiently explore the search space of possible summaries while avoiding premature convergence.

Results & Observations

The system successfully reduced the original document length while preserving important content. Across generations, summaries became more concise and informative, demonstrating the effectiveness of evolutionary optimization for extractive summarization.
